# Detail: every element finished, specific and alive

An element is finished when it has five things:
- a **specific subject**: what exactly it is, named as precisely as its maker would name it, not by its category
- a **material and surface**: what it's made of, and how that surface takes light
- **construction**: how it's built or grown, with the joins, seams and parts that implies
- an **attachment or support**
- one **story detail**: a mark of use, age, ownership or making

No two elements share a drawing.

## Ways to give things character
- **Images within the scene** (art, print, signage, screens): commit to one medium per piece and draw that medium's own
  traits: its line quality, palette limits, edges, surface and how it's mounted. Two pieces in the same medium still
  get different subjects and compositions.
- **Mountings:** show how each thing is held (fixings, frames, hooks, cords, brackets), with the fixing's own
  highlight and shadow.
- **Materials:** each one has its own way of taking light: where the highlight sits and how hard it is, how the edges
  catch light, texture, translucency. Draw that, not a flat fill.
- **Living things:** draw the growth habit of the specific species or breed (how leaves, branches, fur or feathers
  are arranged, and the colour variation within them), not a generic symbol for its kind.
- **Figures and creatures:** friendly, readable faces (a glossy eye with a catchlight beats a glowing one),
  plausible poses (figures.md), and accessories that say who they are.
- **Real places and brands:** invent buildings, landforms and logos. Don't reproduce a recognisable real-world
  landmark or brand unless the brief asks for one. A generic version tends to average toward the most famous
  example of its kind, so push its silhouette away from that on purpose.
- **Text:** real words where text would be legible. Every string fits its container, isn't covered by anything in
  front of it, and reads the right way round. Placeholder words are acceptable only when flagged to the requester.

## Making it alive
- Things that would move, move gently and independently: whatever in the scene breathes, sways, drifts, flows,
  flickers, scrolls or turns.
- Small motion on several layers beats one big motion.
- Use per-element phases and periods, deterministic seeds, and a frame rate cap.

## Using references
Extract the structure (pose, proportions, layout), palette, materials and recurring motifs. Draw fresh: don't trace,
and don't embed watermarked or copyrighted images. Treat personal photos as private reference material.
