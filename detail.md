# Detail: every element finished, specific and alive

An element is finished when it has five things:
- a **specific subject** ("a fly-agaric watercolour", not "a picture")
- a **material and surface** (paper type, wood grain, brushed metal, glaze, fabric pile)
- **construction** (how it's built or grown)
- an **attachment or support**
- one **story detail** (an edition number, a scribbled caption, a chip, a sticker, a worn edge, a name)

No two elements share a drawing.

## Ways to give things character
- **Art, posters and signage:** commit to a medium and draw its traits.
  - tattoo flash: bold outlines, limited palette, banners, stars
  - linocut: carved marks, ink speckle, kraft paper
  - watercolour: soft layered washes, deckled edge, visible underdrawing
  - fine-line ink: hatching, a colour accent, a signature
  - framed print: frame, mat, glazing sheen
  - photo: each one a different image, with a caption or tape
  - die-cut sticker: a white border around its own shape
- **Mountings:** tape with torn ends, pins with a highlight and shadow, clips, frames, hooks with cords, brackets with screws.
- **Materials:** metal gradients with a hard highlight, terracotta with a lit rim, velvet with lighter edges, leather wrap with cross-hatching, stone with speckle, glass with a reflection streak and tinted edges.
- **Plants by species:** succulent rosettes (plump rounded leaves in rings, blushing tips), trailing vines (leaves along a stem), grasses (tapered blades), tree canopies (clustered lobes with a darker underside).
- **Figures and creatures:** friendly, readable faces (a glossy eye with a catchlight beats a glowing one), plausible poses, accessories that say who they are.
- **Text:** real words where text would be legible. Placeholder words are acceptable only when flagged to the requester.

## Making it alive
- Things that would move, move gently and independently: breathing and blinking figures, swaying plants, drifting dust, steam, smoke, water, clouds, flickering flames, scrolling screens, a slowly turning globe or fan.
- Small motion on several layers beats one big motion.
- Use per-element phases and periods, deterministic seeds, and a frame rate cap.

## Using references
Extract the structure (pose, proportions, layout), palette, materials and recurring motifs. Draw fresh: don't trace, and don't embed watermarked or copyrighted images. Treat personal photos as private reference material.
