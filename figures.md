# Figures: build the body, don't hide it

Figures are where one-shot scenes are weakest. Under pressure, agents simplify them into stiff tubes or hide the hard
parts: a back view to dodge the face, a drape over a bent joint, limbs cropped by an occluder. Choosing a back view or a
drape is fine **when it serves the picture**. It is a defect when it is used to avoid drawing the body.

## Construct it, then dress it
1. **Skeleton first.** Place the joints as points: head, neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles. Use
   real proportions (an adult is about 7–7.5 heads tall; the forearm is about the length of the foot; the hip joint
   sits at half height). Draw the skeleton in a debug pass and check it before adding any volume.
2. **Volume on the skeleton.** Build each limb from tapered segments that meet at rounded joints, not from uniform
   tubes. The thigh is thicker than the calf, and the upper arm swells at the shoulder and biceps.
3. **Weight and contact.** Every pose has a support: whatever carries the weight (feet, pelvis, knees, forearms) rests on something.
   Make the contact visible. The seat compresses under the body, and the feet sit flat at the right depth with a
   contact shadow.
4. **Clothing over the forms.** Folds radiate from the points of tension and compression (elbow, knee, hip, armpit).
   Drapery should reveal the form underneath, not replace it.
5. **Then hands and face.**

## Poses that usually go wrong
| pose | the chain that must hold |
|---|---|
| seated | pelvis on the seat; thigh along the seat; the knee bends at the seat edge; the shin goes down to the foot, which lands under or just in front of the knee, never stretched out to a distant point |
| cross-legged | knees out wide; shins crossing in front of the pelvis; the feet tucked under the opposite knees; the back foot partly hidden behind the front shin |
| standing or walking | the weight-bearing leg under the centre of mass; the hips tilt against the shoulders (contrapposto); in a stride the arm swings opposite the leg |
| reaching, throwing or swinging | the line of action runs from the planted foot through the torso to the hand; the shoulder lifts with the arm, and the torso twists toward the action |
| holding things | the fingers wrap whatever is held; the thumb opposes; the wrist bends to suit the grip |

## Hands and faces at small sizes
- **Hands:** mitten shape first, then split the fingers into a group of three or four with a separate thumb, curved
  to the action. A clear silhouette beats drawing every finger.
- **Faces:** place the eye line at half the head, the nose at three-quarters and the mouth just below. Give the head
  a three-quarter turn with the far eye foreshortened. Expression comes from the brows and mouth. One catchlight
  makes the face read as alive.

## Check
- Turn the volumes off and look at the skeleton alone: are the proportions and the joint chain plausible?
- Look at the figure in silhouette only: is the pose readable?
- Hold the pose yourself, or picture it: would you fall over? Where does it hurt?
- Use pose references for the joint chain and the weight. Never trace them.
