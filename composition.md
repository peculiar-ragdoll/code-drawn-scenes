# Composition: placement, spacing, relationships

- **Elements live where they would really be, in sensible relationships:** a chair at its table, a lamp near where light is needed, a rug under the furniture group, a bench facing the view, market stalls along the street, boats in the water. Ask "why is this here?" for each element.
- **The focal subject leads.** It is the brightest, sharpest and most detailed area, with clear space around it. Busy zones get fewer extra props.
- **Use the eye-level band.** Decoration and detail cluster around the horizon and the focal area. Avoid empty middles with everything pushed against the top edge or ceiling. Wide empty areas between major elements are where secondary detail goes.
- **Clusters, not grids.** Group small items (wall art, signage, shelf objects, foliage) with one large anchor, varied sizes and offsets, and smaller pieces orbiting it. Never use a uniform grid unless the subject is a grid (tiles, a window facade).
- **Breathing room.** Leave clear margins, about ¼ of the smaller neighbour's size, around focal points, light sources, openings, screens and faces. Nothing nearly touches unless it's attached.
- **Surfaces read cleanly:** tall items at the back, small ones at the front, gaps between them, nothing overhanging an edge, nothing touching a screen or a light source.
- **Depth layering:** use foreground, mid-ground and background, with overlap to show depth. Place foreground pieces so they frame the scene rather than block the subject, and leave ground in front for them.
- **Balance:** distribute visual weight (dark masses, bright lights, saturated colour) so neither side tips. Repeat each accent colour two or three times across the frame.
- **Frame for the destination:** choose the aspect early (16:9 hero, 9:16 phone, square card). Check that a crop for the main secondary format still contains the subject.
